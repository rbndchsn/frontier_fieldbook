# Red Dead Redemption 2 — The independent frontier naturalist
**Version 2 | 26 November 2026 | Ultimate Edition, Story Mode | Chapters 1–3**

## 1. Purpose and quick-start route
Enjoy a living wilderness, not a second job. Prioritize horses, ecological observation, hunting, financial independence and helping strangers. Use force when required for survival or mandatory objectives; avoid unnecessary harm. No cheats, no online obligations, no completionist pressure. This is a strategy, not a mandate to complete every collectible. Mission names and unlocks are disclosed; plot outcomes and secret locations are not.

**Route:** Finish Chapter 1 → complete Chapter 2's core unlocks and optional content you care about → save before the chapter transition → establish yourself at Clemens Point in Chapter 3 → make a protected free-roam save → stop advancing the yellow story markers whenever you wish. Chapter 2 is also an excellent permanent base. Early Chapter 3 is a recommendation, not an objectively optimal point. You can change your mind.

### Readiness checklist
- [ ] Understand movement, horse controls, map, interaction, cores, weapon wheel, Eagle Eye, Dead Eye and saving.
- [ ] Stables, grooming and legendary-animal map unlocked.
- [ ] Fishing rod unlocked; access to a fence, butcher and trapper established.
- [ ] Reliable bonded horse, saddle, horse reviver, food, ammunition, gun oil and suitable clothing.
- [ ] Bow, varmint rifle and suitable rifle as funds and unlocks allow.
- [ ] Optional: camp leather-working tools, larger satchel and fast travel.
- [ ] Complete any desired chapter-specific companion requests before moving on.
- [ ] Separate protected Chapter 2 and Chapter 3 saves; never overwrite the baselines.

## 2. Chapter 1 — Colter
The prologue is deliberately restrictive. Follow the prompts; don't attempt to turn the snowy tutorial into a wilderness sandbox. Six main missions (some appear concurrently):

| Mission | Learning objective |
|---|---|
| Outlaws from the West | Basic movement, interactions, riding, looting and combat prompts. |
| Enter, Pursued by a Memory | Follow companions; learn horse movement and dealing with wildlife threats. |
| The Aftermath of Genesis | Learn tracking, bow hunting and bringing game back. |
| Old Friends | Learn combat, cover and weapon handling. |
| Who the Hell is Leviticus Cornwall? | Practice scripted mission objectives and following prompts. |
| Eastward Bound | Transition to Horseshoe Overlook and the open world. |

Do not chase gold-medal objectives. They are optional challenges, sometimes at odds with your low-violence style. Some tutorials restrict manual saves; save when free roam becomes available. No need to buy anything in this chapter. [1,2]

This table covers the named Chapter 2 story strands listed in [1,2,3]; exact order and availability depend on preceding missions. Some short setup stages do not appear separately in replay logs. A checklist including optional stages is not a claim every stage is compulsory.

### Before leaving Chapter 2
If interested, talk to camp members regularly and complete Charles's hunting trip, Javier's homestead activity and Lenny's five-finger-fillet activity when offered. Some activities recur in Chapter 3, others do not. Consider the optional personal letter quest, debt tasks and the time-sensitive oil setup. Pearson's rabbit request and Sean's bourbon request are reported to be especially chapter-sensitive; don't spend hours forcing a trigger unless the reward matters to you. Camp requests are opportunistic, not routine administrative duties. [4,5]

### First free-roam excursions
Valentine for supplies and selling; nearby grasslands and river corridors for observing wildlife and practicing hunting. Stay on familiar roads when returning with a valuable carcass. Avoid the red restricted territory near Blackwater; much of the southwest is not safely accessible as Arthur. Explore naturally instead of revealing the whole map online. Save a protected Chapter 2 slot before the final missions.

## 4. Chapter 3 — Clemens Point
A lakeside camp near Rhodes makes an attractive base for a hunter and horseman. Fishing should already be unlocked if you completed the Chapter 2 tutorial. The camp ledger offers a boat upgrade in this chapter. Some Rhodes weapon restrictions are imposed by story state; do not treat the town as a combat playground.

**Recommended protected baseline:** after arrival and completion of The New South, when free roam and saving are available. If you want further unlocks, create a second mid-Chapter 3 baseline after your desired missions, but before Blessed are the Peacemakers. This is deliberately conservative; no claim it is the last possible safe point. You do not need to progress farther to live as a hunter.

### Chapter-specific opportunities
Fishing with Javier and Kieran; dominoes with Tilly; five-finger fillet with Micah; Sean's homestead outing; Bill and Sean coach activities after the relevant unlock. Some requests include Hosea's ginseng, Kieran's burdock root, Molly's mirror and Sadie's harmonica. Take the fishing and conversation opportunities first. Do not force every random trigger. [4–6]

## 5. The practical wilderness routine
Choose an objective for the evening: observe a habitat, find a new route, improve horse bonding, hunt one quality animal, fish, or visit a town. Ride out in daylight initially, set a camp if needed, eat, sleep and return to sell perishables. Keep supplies on hand. No need for a mandatory daily schedule. Random encounters may expire or change if you leave; story missions generally wait for you, but some specific optional events and requests are time-limited.

### Cores and survival
Health, stamina and Dead Eye have outer meters and inner cores. Food, rest and appropriate supplies replenish cores; tonics can replenish or fortify meters. Read item descriptions rather than using valuable tonics casually. Dress for temperature; keep a warm outfit on your horse. Eat enough to avoid sustained underweight, but don't micromanage weight. Carry cooked food, horse food and medicine. Use cover in mandatory combat and leave optional conflict when possible. Clean guns with gun oil if condition falls. Equip desired long guns from the horse before walking away. [7]

### Horse first
Bonding levels 1–4 improve the relationship and capabilities. Ride, lead, calm, pat, brush and feed the horse. A fast horse is not necessarily the best horse for you: prioritize handling, health, stamina and appearance. Carry horse reviver and avoid cliffs, trains, deep water and predator encounters. A horse may die permanently; reviver only helps while the animal is critically injured, not after death. Save before risky trips. Stable spare horses, and do not assume a temporary horse persists indefinitely. Wild horses can be tamed without buying an expensive mount. The Ultimate Edition includes a bonus story horse after relevant stable access; check the stable's available stock and price rather than assuming it appears automatically. Don't seek rare horse locations until you want spoilers.

### Hunting: observe first, harvest selectively
Study animals using binoculars or the focus prompt; read the compendium for habitat and recommended weapon. Eagle Eye reveals trails and scent cues. Approach carefully and use the in-game recommendation for a clean kill. A three-star animal is the usual starting requirement for a perfect pelt; avoid repeated damaging hits. Weapon selection depends on species: small-game arrows for tiny animals, varmint rifle for appropriate small animals, and a suitable rifle or bow for larger game. Do not assume a single weapon covers every species. Legendary pelts follow different quality rules. Legendary hunting is optional; the tutorial map does not mean every legendary animal is accessible at once. [8,9]

Use the horse to carry carcasses and pelts; large carcasses consume the main rear cargo position. Carcasses decay, so sell or donate promptly. Keep perfect crafting pelts separate in your decision-making: Pearson's satchel requirements and the trapper's recipes are separate. You may sell a needed pelt to the wrong vendor and have to hunt another. Selling to the trapper registers materials for his recipes; crafting also costs money. A butcher is for cash, Pearson for camp supplies/crafting, a trapper for cash and specialized gear, a fence for valuables and certain crafted trinkets. Legendary pelts lost before delivery generally become available to the trapper for crafting, but you forfeit the sale proceeds; do not rely on that as a normal transport method.

### Fishing and plants
Use the fishing tutorial, then experiment with bait/lures suited to the water and species. Fishing can be a peaceful activity and cash source; release fish you don't need if you like. Gather plants as you travel, consult compendium and recipe requirements, and craft useful provisions rather than collecting compulsively. Legendary fish use a separate quest and special lures. No need to look up secret coordinates.

## 6. Economy: independence rather than grinding
| Activity | Purpose | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Hunting and fishing | Renewable leisure income and provisions | Modest cash per outing, transport and spoilage |
| Treasure maps | Large one-time rewards | Clues, exploration, finite treasure |
| Story missions | Major rewards and unlocks | Mandatory conflict in some missions |
| Bounties | Paid side activity | Risk; capture requirements |
| Selling found valuables | Cash at fence | Not every item is safe to sell; keep crafting materials |
| Camp contribution | Supplies, honor, upgrades | Optional expenditure |
| Robbery | Potential cash | Honor loss, bounties, injuries and opportunity costs |

Gold bars sell for $500 each at a fence. Treasure and main missions typically outperform routine hunting for cash; hunting remains the preferred lifestyle, not a claim of maximum dollars/hour. Do not sell unique crafting materials without checking recipes. Keep a small emergency reserve (suggested $100–200 after essential purchases; subjective, not a game requirement). Prioritize horse reviver, food, gun oil, suitable hunting weapons, ammunition and weather clothing over luxury gear. Avoid unnecessary bounties. Pay a bounty at a post office when practical; a bounty is distinct from an active wanted search. You cannot buy businesses for passive income in Story Mode. [3,7,9]

### Camp upgrades: only if useful
After ledger unlock, leather-working tools cost $225 and enable Pearson's satchel crafting subject to other requirements. Dutch's lodging upgrade ($220) followed by Arthur's ($325) unlocks fast travel. The combined $545 is optional; the point of the game is often the journey. Upgrade supplies only if you use them. A larger satchel is convenient, not a prerequisite to exploration. Chapter 3 boat is optional. Do not treat camp donations as rent. [3,10]

## 7. Honor, people, risk
Greet people, help stranded or injured strangers, avoid unnecessary civilian harm, return favors when possible, and spare people when offered a choice. Some strangers are hostile or deceptive; read the situation rather than assuming every request is genuine. Honor affects selected prices, dialogue and story outcomes; it is not a perfect measure of ethics. High honor is a preference, not a requirement for all gameplay. Mandatory story violence exists; completing the story completely nonviolently is not realistic. Avoid initiating confrontations just for money. If a wanted level appears, leave the search area when possible, avoid escalation and deal with the bounty afterward. Some random encounters and camp requests offer later callbacks; enjoy them without tracking everything.

## 8. Exploration without spoilers
Observe habitat transitions between forest, prairie, mountain, wetland and river. Study animals, plants and horses for the compendium; inspect unusual landmarks for journal sketches. Look at maps and documents you find, follow promising trails, and occasionally visit places at different times. Hidden treasures, strange structures, fossils, rock carvings, dreamcatchers, rare wildlife and mysterious encounters exist. Some secrets are gated by time, conditions or chapter. Do not confuse an in-game ecology with a scientifically validated model; wildlife respawns and populations are simplified. Avoid full interactive maps on your first run. Use a specific walkthrough only if stuck. [7,8,11]

## 9. Save system and safe branching
Pause → Story → Save Game when permitted. Autosave is useful but not a substitute for manual checkpoints. Save restrictions can apply during missions, combat or other active events. Do not enable cheats if you want normal saving and achievements. Suggested slot scheme (the game may display mission names rather than custom labels): 1 Chapter 2 arrival; 2 Chapter 2 before transition; 3 early Chapter 3 protected; 4 mid-Chapter 3 protected if desired; 5 active wilderness; 6 active story. The labels are your own notes, not guaranteed in-game custom names. Keep autosave enabled unless switching branches causes confusion; explicitly load the correct manual slot before starting and save into that branch afterward. Independent progress does not merge. Keep the protected slots unchanged. If Highball or Rockstar cloud sync shows a conflict, check timestamp and local progress before choosing a version. Google Drive notes are not automatic game-save backups. Do not move live save files until the exact Highball prefix and cloud behavior have been verified.

## 10. Minimal troubleshooting and session checklist
Play Story Mode, not Online. The M4 Max has substantial hardware headroom, but Highball compatibility, controller mapping and actual frame rate must be tested. Start with default graphics and a moderate output resolution, then adjust; don't assume CrossOver-specific settings apply to Highball. Use TV Game Mode and direct HDMI if available. Bluetooth controller should connect to Mac; check actual in-game inputs. Keep the Mac awake during downloads and use Steam bandwidth limits if other devices need the connection. If a mission seems missing, check prerequisites, camp dialogue, letters, the map and time of day; don't immediately restart. If save is unavailable, finish or leave the active encounter safely. Before quitting, return to safe free roam and manually save. 

**One rule above all:** when you feel like hunting, hunt; when you feel like a story, play a mission. No spreadsheet, fixed quota or required journal entry. The personal journal is optional.
